# Noridoc: ui/organisms

Path: @/ui/organisms

### Overview

- The third presentational layer of the Shemá design system: stateful compositions that consume @/domain state via props/hooks and own the interaction-critical surfaces the listener touches. This folder ships the **necklace**, the **seam modal**, the **block done**, **break suggestion**, **goal reached**, **nav footer** and **preparing session** organisms (exported from @/ui/organisms/index.ts) plus the out-of-barrel organisms — the shell-only **connection gate** and **tutorial popup** (consumed by @/ui/app via direct path) and the two Triage organisms (ENG-225): the **Triage picker** and the facilitator-only **coverage drawer** (consumed by the Triage station via direct path), plus the dashboard's **session list**.
- **Since ENG-689 (scope cut 1/4) this folder has no interview surface left.** The conversation stage, the storyteller guide, the conversation mode picker and the artifact cards were deleted along with the Conversation/Report/Export stations that composed them (@/ui/pages) — the app now runs Ouvir → Cortar → Triagem → Frases → Rever (ENG-725) and stops, and generates no artifact. Every mention of them below is history, kept where a still-live decision (a Radix convention, a barrel-export precedent, a z-index scale) was set by one of them and inherited by what remains.
- The **necklace** (`ui/organisms/necklace`, ENG-220) is the hero "colar" (redesign §6) that renders the whole story as a wrapped field of beads: the full bead-state vocabulary (palette-tinted segment beads, square end beads for locked scene ends, a soft selection band with emphasized edge beads, a dim + dashed scene-band over the active-scene window in Segmentação) plus its own click model, hover-edge preview and 60 fps playback lighting.
- The **connection gate** (`ui/organisms/connection-gate`, ENG-224) is the online-only editing shield (PRD §7.3/§13): `ConnectionGate({ online, children })` renders its children always mounted and, when offline, adds a PT-BR `role=status` banner plus a blocking overlay over the editing surface. It is a pure organism (no adapters — `online` arrives resolved by the shell).
- The **seam modal** (`ui/organisms/seam-modal`, ENG-228) is the border-crossing dialog (PRD v2 §8.6; redesign §6.5, prototype "1g — a costura desliza"): a full-olive panel showing a small stretch of the cord with today's border and the proposed new one, offering exactly the choices the domain classified. The Segmentação station will open it when a phrase selection crosses the scene border (wiring lands with ENG-237).
- The **tutorial popup** (`ui/organisms/tutorial-popup`, ENG-231) is the facilitator's per-station tips chrome (PRD v2 §7.5): a cream card of one short PT-BR tip per station, anchored to a round "?" trigger in the shell's bottom-right overlay layer. It is mounted only by the shell addon @/ui/app/addons/tutorial.tsx (never by a station page); its dismissal semantics and localStorage key are documented in @/ui/organisms/tutorial-popup/README.md.
- The **Triage picker** (`ui/organisms/triage-picker`, ENG-225, rebuilt in ENG-390, PRD v2 §8.5) is the scene-kind chooser: all 27 kinds in one alphabetically sorted grid inside its own scroll box, an always-visible dashed "Nenhum se encaixa" card outside that box, and a confidence step that replaces the grid once a kind is chosen. The grid's columns come from `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(184px, 1fr))`, so it spends whatever width the station hands it — three columns inside the organism's own 640px cap, four when the station lies on its side (@/ui/pages/triage). In the confidence step the trio and "Confirmar" share one line, which is what keeps the button on screen in a short window; an empty `::before` gutter mirrors the one the button sits in, so revealing it does not slide the trio sideways under the pointer, and the trio's cards give up width before the line ever wraps. The **coverage drawer** (`ui/organisms/coverage-drawer`, "Cobertura · só facilitadora") is a right-side olive panel built on Radix Dialog, closed by default and invisible to the listener until the facilitator opens it by the vertical tab.
- The **session list** (`ui/organisms/session-list`, ENG-222; redesigned to the Shemá v2 prototype in ENG-278) is the dashboard's grid of sessions (PRD v2 §7.2): a `<ul aria-label="sessões">` of story cards, each with a **bead-thumbnail cover** (the §7.2 glanceable progress: `Pearl` atoms lit in proportion to the saved step), the story title, a `slug · project` line, a status label mirrored on `data-status` (one hyphenated value per @/contracts lifecycle status — in progress, preparing the review, concluded — resolved through @/ui/i18n by a key map the organism owns), "Editado <última modificação>", and ONE primary action ("Retomar" or "Abrir"). An optional `onNew` prepends the dashed "Comece uma nova história" card, which doubles as the empty state. A facilitator surface — normal density, digits allowed.
- The **nav footer** (`ui/organisms/nav-footer`, protótipo v3 §1) is the persistent bottom navigation bar, in the trimmed form the owner asked for: **back on the left, advance on the right, nothing in the middle**. Since ENG-725 `StationNav` also takes an optional `aside` — a station's own functional content rendered in the back slot when there is no back (the Rever's legend of the necklace marks); it is not the centre context label the owner removed (the centre stays empty), and stations that do not pass it render exactly as before. It splits the work by page — the body keeps that page's own commands ("✓ Confirmar esta cena", "Nenhum se encaixa"), the footer keeps only what changes page. Three exports: `NavFooterProvider` + `NavFooterOutlet` (the shell lends the place, @/ui/app/App.tsx) and `StationNav` (a station renders its own navigation into that place through a **portal**). A station that renders no `StationNav` — login, dashboard, waiting screens, the granularity lock — simply has no footer, so the shell needs no exception list. `renderStation` (@/ui/organisms/nav-footer/testing.tsx) gives page tests the minimal shell.
- The **goal reached** screen (`ui/organisms/goal-reached`, ENG-653) is the celebration for today's goal (v4 prototype, "META ALCANÇADA"): a full-screen scrim over the station with four beads (the last a rounded square), one italic line, one supporting line, and two pill buttons — "Seguir mais um pouco" (closes, work continues in place) and "Guardar por hoje" (leaves to the dashboard). Sister to the break suggestion and built the same way, with two differences: the primary action here is the one that STAYS, and the surface sits above the block-closing screen in the stacking order (`z-index: 57`, the prototype's own value).
- The **break suggestion** (`ui/organisms/break-suggestion`, ENG-650) is the once-per-session rest suggestion (PRD-redesign prototype, "PAUSA SUGERIDA"): a full-screen scrim over the station with five beads, one italic line, one supporting line and two pill buttons — "Fazer uma pausa" (leaves to the dashboard) and "Seguir mais um pouco" (closes and leaves the person exactly where they were). It suggests and never blocks. Unlike the other organisms it owns a small piece of time: the 45-minute threshold, the once-per-session latch, and the guard against interrupting something in flight.

- The **block done** screen (`ui/organisms/block-done`, ENG-651; extended by ENG-725) is the screen that closes a block of work (PRD-redesign prototype, "FIM DE BLOCO"): it marks that a block just ended before the next one begins. Its `block` prop type, `ClosedBlock`, has two values — `'triagem'` (triage closing) and `'historia'` (the story concluding at @/ui/pages/review, ENG-725, the flow's actual end since then); phrasing closing stopped being a block in ENG-725, since the Review is simply the next station. Full-screen and **opaque olive**, with no scrim and no blur: unlike the other two full-screen surfaces it does not let the station behind show through. Five beads whose colours come from the data, a PER-BLOCK eyebrow, headline + subtitle naming the block that ENDED, and two pill buttons whose labels are also per-block (`blockDone.<block>.eyebrow/headline/subtitle/primary/secondary` in @/ui/i18n) — at `'triagem'` the primary continues into Frases and "Guardar e descansar" leaves to the dashboard; at `'historia'` the primary ("Voltar às histórias") leaves to the dashboard and the secondary ("Olhar de novo") just closes the screen, revealing the Review still mounted behind it with its panorama intact. It owns no time and no latch at all: `block` arrives from the shell, which is what knows a boundary was crossed. Since ENG-653 it also takes `busy` and `onOpenChange`, the same pair the other two full-screen surfaces use; `open` is derived at render (`block !== null && !busy`), which is what makes waiting a deferral rather than a loss — a block closes once, so a swallowed screen would never return.

- All of these are presentational — props in, events out. Unlike @/ui/atoms and @/ui/molecules, organisms may import @/domain **types and pure helpers**, may pull their copy from @/ui/i18n (`useTranslation`) and may hold interaction state; they still never import adapters.
- **The barrel @/ui/organisms/index.ts exports only some organisms** (necklace, seam modal, preparing session, nav footer, break suggestion, goal reached, block done). Connection gate, tutorial popup, Triage picker, coverage drawer and session list are deliberately NOT exported: editing the barrel was outside their issues' scope (precedent set by ConnectionGate/ENG-224). Consumers import them by direct path, or the consuming station issue adds the export.

### How it fits into the larger codebase

- Sits between @/ui/molecules and the wiring layers (@/ui/pages, @/ui/templates, @/ui/app). Consumers import from the barrel where an export exists, by direct path otherwise; sibling organisms always import each other by direct path (never the barrel) to avoid cycles — the same rule atoms and molecules follow.
- The dependency direction is enforced mechanically by @/.dependency-cruiser.cjs, not convention: organisms may import @/domain (types and pure helpers), @/ui/tokens (`PaletteEntry`, `scenePalette`) and the @/ui/atoms / @/ui/molecules barrels; the ban on adapter imports is a required CI check.
- **Selection decisions stay in the domain.** The necklace reports a raw bead index through callbacks (`onBeadPointerDown`, `onEdgeHover`, `onHeadTap`) and never decides what a click means — the click model lives in @/domain/selection.ts. Likewise the Triage picker never mutates triage state: it only emits the contract-ready values (`onConfirm(kind, confidence)` / `onNoneFit()`) for the station to feed into @/domain/triage.ts, and the coverage drawer only displays a `Coverage` computed by @/domain/coverage.ts.
- **Organisms are where presentational tokens become contract values.** The ConfidenceTrio molecule emits `certeza`/`quase`/`duvida`; the map to the stored `alta`/`média`/`baixa` (@/domain `Confidence`) lives in the Triage picker, so no page ever converts. English `scene_kind` values pass through untouched — the **display** label comes from `sceneKindLabel` (@/ui/i18n/scene-kind-label.ts), which resolves `skShort` (PT) or `skEnShort` (EN) from @/domain/scene-kinds.ts. The stored value is the contract and never follows the UI language.
- **The seam modal is the interpreter surface for the effects-as-data `BorderOffer`** from @/domain/seam.ts (see @/domain/docs.md): `classifyBorderMove` returns the offer as a pure value and the modal renders EXACTLY the option set that value encodes — `offer.kind` picks the variant (simple / escalation / two-productive) and `offer.canMove` gates "Mover mesmo assim". The modal never computes thresholds; the station will wire the three callbacks back into `moveBorder`/`reanchorFrase`/back-to-Triage.
- **Radix is used headlessly** (scoped packages: `@radix-ui/react-dialog` — introduced by the seam modal/ENG-228, reused by the coverage drawer, block done and the dashboard's card dialogs — and `@radix-ui/react-popover` for the tutorial popup and the dashboard's actions menu): behavior/ARIA primitives only (focus trap, ESC, `aria-modal`, scroll lock, anchor positioning); all styling stays the plain-CSS `.cds-*` convention described in @/ui/docs.md.
- `Span` (the `{ s, e }` bead range) is the shared coordinate between the necklace and @/domain — segments, selection, the active-scene window and locked-end beads all arrive as bead indices, matching the "bead indices are the universal coordinate" invariant in @/domain.
- The **session list** is the Dashboard station's (@/ui/pages/dashboard) only consumer: since ENG-281 its actions menu is a full "Renomear a história"/"Apagar a história" menu on every card (the download entry the prototype's kebab also showed left with the artifacts in ENG-689). The organism stays out of it: the menu arrives as the opaque `menu` node on `SessionCardData`, so the grid knows nothing about ports, renaming or dialogs (reconciliation table in @/ui/pages/dashboard/docs.md).
- **The session card takes its progress pre-chewed, never raw session state**: `SessionCardData` carries `progress` (0..1), `progressLabel` (the accessible name of the cover) and an already-formatted `lastModified` string — the organism does no date and no step arithmetic, @/ui/pages/dashboard derives both (`formatWhen`, `progressOf`). This replaced `SessionStationGlance`, a hand-copy of the shell's `StepperStationView` shape that existed only because organisms may not import the wiring layer (@/ui/app); the bead cover needs no shell type at all, so the mirror is gone.

### Core Implementation

- **The goal celebration derives "open"; it does not set it** (@/ui/organisms/goal-reached/goal-reached.tsx). Its only state is `dismissed`; being open is computed in render as `reached && !busy && !dismissed`. That is not a style preference: opening from inside an effect via `setState` is a cascading render and `react-hooks` rejects it as an error, and the derived form gives the deferral rule for free — a goal crossed while the mic is open (or while another full-screen surface is up) opens by itself on the render after `busy` clears, without the bar having to cross the mark again. The chime is the one true side effect and fires once ever, guarded by a ref rather than by the effect's dependencies, because the shell's `chime` prop is a fresh closure on every one of ITS renders. Everything else follows the break suggestion's conventions: Radix Dialog for `role="dialog"`, a real focus trap and Escape; Escape and every other Radix dismissal route to "Seguir mais um pouco"; initial focus is forced onto that same button, because an accidental Enter must never take someone away from the question they were on. Copy comes from @/ui/i18n (`goalReached.*`) and carries no digits — both people see this screen (§9.2), and the counting stays on the facilitator's Setup card.
- **`onOpenChange` on both full-screen organisms** (ENG-653). Each reports whether it is on screen so the shell can feed the other's `busy`. Without it the exclusion is one-directional: `busy` alone stops a NEW surface from covering something in flight, but a self-latching organism cannot see a sibling that is already up.
- **The block done screen keeps NO state — that is the whole design** (@/ui/organisms/block-done/block-done.tsx). Nothing here decides when to appear: `block` is a prop, `null` renders nothing, and the two callbacks go straight out. The reason is the failure mode the issue names: an implementation that keys off "which station am I on" replays the screen on every resume. What actually opens it is a boundary being **crossed** (see @/ui/app/docs.md), which no component below the shell can observe. Built on Radix Dialog for `role="dialog"`, a real focus trap and Escape. Escape routes to **`onContinue`** — the mirror image of the break suggestion's choice, and for the same reason: the station the person just reached is already mounted behind this screen, so continuing is the move that leaves nobody anywhere they did not ask to be, and initial focus sits on that same primary button so an accidental Enter agrees with an accidental Escape. The five beads reuse the `Pearl` atom (the last one `sceneEnd`, square on the cord) and take their tints from a `tints` prop — the scenes' own colours both after Triagem and after the story concludes (ENG-725: @/ui/app/App.tsx always hands the shell's `scenePalette` slice keyed by scene count, not a phrase palette) — cycling when there are fewer colours than beads. Copy comes from @/ui/i18n (`blockDone.*`, keyed per block) and carries no digits (§9.2).
- **A full-screen surface needs a z-index, and this one learned it the hard way** (@/ui/organisms/block-done/block-done.css). `position: fixed; inset: 0` covers the viewport geometrically but says nothing about paint order: the shell's header is `sticky; z-index: 30` and the addons layer is `fixed; z-index: 20`, so with `z-index: auto` this screen rendered UNDER both — "← Histórias" and the tutorial "?" sat on top of it, offering exits the screen does not offer. It now declares `z-index: 56`, the value @/docs/design/prototype.html gives this screen on line 843 (the suggested break is 57 on that same scale, which is also the order the exclusivity implies). **No DOM test catches this class of bug**, and worse, the obvious probe lies: Radix sets `pointer-events: none` on the rest of the page, so `elementFromPoint` answers "the screen is on top" while the header paints over it. It was found by screenshotting the running app, and the regression guard in `block-done.test.tsx` compares against the REAL `header.css`/`addons-layer.css`, so raising the header without raising this screen fails the suite. Note the same omission exists in @/ui/organisms/break-suggestion (no z-index declared) — not touched here, since it is another issue's file.

- **The break suggestion is a poll, not an alarm** (@/ui/organisms/break-suggestion/break-suggestion.tsx). A `setInterval` of `BREAK_SETTLE_MS` (4 s, the prototype's own step) checks whether `BREAK_AFTER_MS` (45 min) has elapsed since the mount; while the `busy` prop is true no interval runs at all. That shape answers both halves of the "never interrupt" rule with one mechanism: a threshold that passes mid-recording is **deferred, never swallowed**, and when `busy` clears the effect re-arms and the first check lands one step later — so the suggestion never appears in the same instant the recording stops or the next screen paints. The whole lifecycle is a three-state latch (`armed` → `open` → `spent`); `spent` is terminal, so either button, or Escape, ends the suggestion for that mount. Resetting it is the shell's `key={sessionId}` remount, not an internal rule. Built on Radix Dialog, so it gets `role="dialog"`, a real focus trap and Escape for free — the prototype markup has no ARIA at all, which is a prototype limitation, not a decision. Escape and every other Radix dismissal route to "Seguir mais um pouco": leaving by accident would cost the person the question they were on. Initial focus is forced onto the secondary button (the seam-modal precedent, APG): an accidental Enter must never leave the station. Copy comes from @/ui/i18n (`breakSuggestion.*`) and carries no digits — §9.2 means the screen never says "45 minutes".

- **Geometry is a pure port** in @/ui/organisms/necklace/geometry.ts — a 1:1 translation of the reference `renderCord`/`drawBand`/`beadAtXY` (see @/docs/reference/index.html). It has zero DOM/framework imports: `resolveWindow` (active scene ± `max(3, round(2/beadSec))` margin, or the whole story when no scene is active), `beadsPerRow` (from container width), `beadPosition` (absolute left/top of a bead), `beadAtXY` (pointer coords → clamped bead index), `bandRects` (one rectangle per row when a band wraps across the row break), `cordRects` (the thin cord line drawn behind each row, port of the Protótipo's `_rowStyle`: one 2px rect per row, spanning bead-center to bead-center), and `centerOffset` (owner feedback: the necklace stays centered — half the slack between the measured container width and the widest row, `min(count, bpr)` beads, never negative). The pure math is unit-tested independently of the component.
- **Two centerings, and they compose.** `centerOffset` centers the whole FIELD in the container; `rowShift` (prototype v3 §4) centers each ROW inside that field, so the last incomplete row no longer hangs left. `rowShift` measures against the field width (`min(count, bpr)` beads), NOT the container — measuring against the container would double-center a necklace short enough to fit one row and push it right, which is why a single-row necklace returns 0. Every consumer applies it: `beadPosition`, `cordRects` and `bandRects` add it when drawing, and `beadAtXY` resolves the ROW from Y first and subtracts that row's shift from X before resolving the column. Adding it on one side only is the classic way to desync a click from what is drawn.
- **`centerOffset` reaches every geometry consumer, not just layout.** `computeField` adds it to every bead's `left`, to the scene/selection band rects and to `cordRects` (via a shared `shift` helper) so the rendered field is always centered; the pointer handler subtracts the SAME offset from `ev.clientX` before calling `beadAtXY`, so hit-testing stays correct against the shifted beads. Getting either side wrong (adding it to draw but not to hit-testing, or vice versa) desyncs clicks from what's drawn — this is why the browser click tests click the CENTER of each bead's `[data-idx]` element rather than a raw computed coordinate, so they stay correct regardless of the current centering math.
- **Size is a per-station prop, not a constant.** @/ui/organisms/necklace/geometry.ts exports the `Size` shape (`slot`/`bead`/`row`) plus the Protótipo-matched presets `SIZE_L` (bead 26px, the Escutas) and `SIZE_SEG` (bead 30px, Segmentação) alongside the pre-existing default; all are re-exported from @/ui/organisms/index.ts so a page picks its size with `<Necklace size={SIZE_L} .../>` instead of the organism hardcoding one bead size for every station. `SIZE_EXPORT` (bead 22px) was the Export station's preset and sat unused between ENG-689 (which removed that station) and ENG-725, which gave it a new reader: @/ui/pages/review renders the whole story at this size.
- **`phraseEndBeads` (ENG-725)** is an optional structural prop, alongside `segments`/`lockedEndBeads`, marking which bead indices close a phrase rather than a scene; it flows through `computeField` into each bead's `BeadDescriptor.phraseEnd`, which the `Pearl` atom (@/ui/atoms/docs.md) renders as its own square mark, distinct from a scene end. Like every other structural prop it must stay referentially stable across renders — a fresh array on every render would defeat the field's `React.memo`. Stations that never pass it (listen, cut, triage, phrases) render exactly as before; @/ui/pages/review is the first caller, since it is the only station that shows scene ends and phrase ends on the same field at once.
- **Imperative island for playback lighting** (@/docs/architecture.md §6): the bead field is a `React.memo` component that receives ONLY structural props — `playbackHead` is deliberately excluded, so feeding a new head does NOT re-render bead elements. A `useLayoutEffect` toggles a `data-play` attribute (`played`/`head`) on bead wrappers via the container ref. DOM node identity stays stable across head changes, so per-frame updates never pass through React state or reconciliation.
- **The beads scroll in their own window, and playback follows the lit bead** (ENG-387). `Necklace` renders a `.cds-necklace-window` wrapper around the positioned bead container; an optional `maxHeight` prop (px, per mount — Escuta 1 asks for 9 rows, Escuta 2 for 8, Triagem's scene-reminder card for 5 and Segmentação for 6, each written as `n × size.row + 12`) caps it, and that wrapper is the scroll container, so a long story never pushes the title, the play button or the confirmation off the page. Without the prop the field renders whole and nothing scrolls. The follow decision is the pure `followScrollTop(beadTop, size, viewport, scrollTop)` in @/ui/organisms/necklace/geometry.ts: it returns `null` when the lit bead is already visible — which both stops the window fighting someone who scrolled somewhere deliberately and stops a scroll being issued on every single bead — and otherwise centers the bead, clamped at 0. `beadTop` is the bead's CENTRE, so visibility counts half a bead on each side. The call site lives INSIDE the imperative playback effect, never in the memoized field's props, and reads `prefers-reduced-motion` through `globalThis.matchMedia` at call time (a stylesheet media query cannot reach an imperative `scrollTo`; `globalThis` because the component's `window` identifier is the active-scene prop). The container's `touch-action` is `pan-y`, not `none`, so a finger can scroll the window while the horizontal boundary drag still works. The window keeps two defences against its own scrollbar, and they answer different scrollbars (ENG-506): `scrollbar-gutter: stable` reserves the gutter a CLASSIC bar would steal — without it the bar narrows the container, which changes beads-per-row, which changes the height, which makes the bar come and go — and a `padding-right` gives the OVERLAY bar (macOS) room, since it is painted over the content and reserves nothing, leaving the last column of beads under it where a touch hits the scroller instead. Both live on the scrolling window and never on the measured `.cds-necklace`: that inner element's `clientWidth` (padding included) is what decides beads-per-row, and the beads anchor to its padding box, so padding there would move the maths without moving the beads and split the drawn positions from the hit-test. Since ENG-670 that same window also styles the bar itself: `::-webkit-scrollbar { width: 24px }`, the WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 (Target Size — Minimum) floor for a target that has to be hit without precision — not the 44px of an isolated tap target, because a scrollbar is an EDGE target as tall as the window and only its width is scarce, and 44px would eat 12% of a 360px phone. It is thicker for everyone, deliberately not behind `@media (pointer: coarse)`: on touch the finger drags the CONTENT, so that query would exclude precisely the pointer users who actually grab the bar. Only the `-webkit-` pseudo-element is declared, never `scrollbar-width`/`scrollbar-color` — in Chromium the standard properties DISABLE the custom scrollbar and the px width is lost — so in engines without the pseudo-element (Firefox) the bar stays as it was, which is the overlay case the `padding-right` already covers. The thumb is `color-mix(in srgb, currentColor …)` with a 4px transparent border and `background-clip: content-box`: the hand grabs the full 24px while the paint stays at 16, and `currentColor` follows whatever surface the window lands on (cream, the olive ceremonial screens, dark) with no override hook. The `padding-right` stays at 16px: it measures the macOS OVERLAY bar, which the OS draws at its own size and which the 24px gutter does not cover; where the custom bar takes its own space instead, those 16px become the breathing room that stops a finger aiming at the last bead from grazing the bar.
- **One delegated native pointer handler**: a single `pointerdown`/`pointermove`/`pointerleave` listener is attached with `addEventListener` on the container inside a mount-once `useLayoutEffect` (NOT React's `onPointerDown`, which delegates at the document root). Pointer → bead is pure geometry (`beadAtXY` over `getBoundingClientRect`), clamped to the render window.
- **Ref-mirror for live props**: because the listeners mount once, they read the latest props through `ixRef`, written inside a layout effect (NOT during render — the `react-hooks/refs` rule forbids ref mutation in render). This keeps the handlers current without re-attaching them.
- **Head-tap precedence** (redesign §6.2, "the glowing head pauses"): when the tapped bead equals the glowing `playbackHead`, `onHeadTap` fires instead of `onBeadPointerDown`.
- **The nav footer is a PORTAL, not state in the provider.** The first cut published the station's navigation through an effect into provider state; that left the footer one render behind the station, and for a frame it showed the previous label ("Pronto com esta cena" when the scene was already done) — a real hazard (a click on the stale button acts on the previous screen) that also made the e2e helpers' synchronous `count()` probes flaky. `StationNav` now renders `createPortal(<footer/>, slotNode)`, so the footer commits together with the station and the handlers are always the current ones. The only extra render is the callback ref registering the slot, once at mount. All five surviving stations publish through it — Escuta 1, Cortar, Triagem, Frases, Rever (ENG-725 added the fifth, "Concluir a história"); the Conversation/Export exception this bullet used to carry (per-question nav and an in-flight-disabled "Guardar os documentos" staying in the body) left with those stations in ENG-689. From @/docs/design/prototype-v3.md only the §3 empty-state link ("usar cortes de exemplo") is deliberately NOT ported: it seeds fabricated cuts, which is a prototype demo affordance, not something a real session should be able to write.
- **`onDragBoundary` fires on every bead crossed, plus once more with `done`** when the finger lifts (never on `pointercancel` — the gesture was taken, not finished). The organism still knows nothing about audio; `done` exists because the PAGE needs a single moment to make sound at, since sounding on every bead of a drag would be a racket. The page also has to stash where the boundary started, because the domain overwrites it on the first step.
- **The click does NOT silence the edge dwell** — faithful to the reference (L584–597), where `pointerdown` never touches the hover timer. The organism still arms and fires it exactly as the reference does; what changed (2026-08-07, owner) is that the PAGE refuses to act on it while sound is in progress — see `playHoverEdge` (@/ui/pages/cut/cutting.ts). Two earlier attempts suppressed the dwell by pointer POSITION (#164 by bead, #172 by edge zone) and both were dropped: precedence between sound and hover is the rule that actually holds, and it lives where the player lives, not in the organism, which knows nothing about audio.
- The `window` prop is the ACTIVE SCENE span; the organism derives the render window internally, dims the margin beads (`state='dim'`) and draws the dashed scene-band over the scene. `transportOnly` (Escuta/review) suppresses all selection affordances.
- **The seam modal is purely presentational and controlled** (@/ui/organisms/seam-modal): props are the `BorderOffer | null` (type-only @/domain import; `null` renders nothing), the scene and its immediate neighbor as `{ span, tint }` cord data (`SeamCordSide`; `null` neighbor = the border only stretches the scene), plus the outcome callbacks `onMove`/`onReanchor`/`onGoTriage`. A small pure strip model windows a few beads around the seam, clamps to the given spans and zones each bead as scene / overshoot / neighbor; the overshoot beads get a light core over the scene tint and the cream selection halo from prototype 1g.
- **The Radix policy** (decided 2026-07-09; applied by the seam modal's and the coverage drawer's `@radix-ui/react-dialog` and the tutorial popup's `@radix-ui/react-popover`): Radix contributes BEHAVIOR only — focus trap, ESC, dismissal, anchor positioning — while every visual stays 100% Shemá tokens via the usual `.cds-*` CSS. In the seam modal, ESC and overlay click both funnel through Radix `onOpenChange(false)` → `onReanchor`, the safe default that only discards the selection (§9.5: guide, never punish).
- **The tutorial popup deliberately skips `Popover.Portal`**: the content renders inline so it stays INSIDE the shell's `.cds-addons-layer` (@/ui/app/addons-layer.tsx) instead of escaping to `document.body` — the requirement is overlay-layer chrome that never lands in the station's `main`. Radix's internal Popper still positions the card `position: fixed` relative to the trigger even without a portal. It auto-opens on mount WITHOUT stealing focus (`onOpenAutoFocus` prevented — it is non-modal status chrome), and dismissal has two levels: X/ESC/click-outside hide it while a session stays loaded (component `useState`; the addon stays mounted across stations, so the choice survives station changes — but leaving to the dashboard unmounts the popup, and another session re-offers the tips), while "Não mostrar de novo" persists via localStorage. The "?" trigger remains as the re-entry route in both cases — dismissing never costs the information. An unknown station key renders nothing, and the tips carry zero digits (same §9.2 sweep as the other listener-adjacent surfaces).
- **Seam-slide preview is static-first**: both markers — "borda de hoje" (dashed) and "borda nova" (ember-glow) — always render, so the before/after states are visible without motion; the slide animation of the new border lives only inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, CSS-source-asserted in tests exactly like the necklace. Marker order mirrors the crossing side (`crossEnd` vs `crossStart`).
- **The Triage picker is a two-stage machine.** Stage 1 is every `SCENE_KINDS` entry in one 3-column grid, sorted by `localeCompare` on the **displayed** label so the order follows the UI language, inside `.cds-triage-picker-scroll` — the only scrolling region — with the dashed none-fit card rendered outside that box but still inside the radiogroup, so it never needs a scroll to be found. ENG-390 deleted the earlier "Mais comuns" + by-theme disclosure design: the first real walkthrough showed the disclosure was too discreet to find, so people classified from the short list believing it was the whole list, and the thematic grouping forced them to guess a bucket (our taxonomy, not theirs) before they could look for a kind. Picking a kind swaps the entire grid for stage 2: the chosen chip (Pearl + PT label, EN in `title`), a "Trocar tipo" escape hatch back to stage 1, and the ConfidenceTrio — choosing there only SELECTS (local state, real `aria-checked`); the contract emission happens on the explicit "Confirmar" button revealed after the first choice, because the trio is a standard APG radiogroup where arrow keys move-and-select, and select-equals-commit would let keyboard exploration commit the triage.
- **The grid has to say that it continues** (ENG-390, second half). Flattening the list removed the too-discreet disclosure, but it put all 27 kinds in a scroll region — and at 1280×900 exactly 12 are visible. A region that scrolls in silence recreates the original defect in a new shape: a short list in view, the rest invisible, no cue. Two things answer it. First, the scrollbar is never hidden (`scrollbar-width` stays at the system default) — but that is not enough on its own, because macOS overlay scrollbars only appear _while_ scrolling. Second, `.cds-triage-picker-more`: a `position: sticky; bottom: -2px` veil, pinned to the visible bottom edge, that fades the last row into the page background. It is an **element on top of the cards, not a background on the scroll box** — the classic `background-attachment: local` scroll shadow paints _behind_ content, and these 27 cards are opaque, so (measured in a real browser) that shadow only showed in the 9px gutters. The `-2px` matters: `sticky` aligns to the inside of the padding, and the 2px focus-ring breathing room would leave an unveiled sliver of card at the very edge. A negative `margin-top` makes the veil lie over the last row rather than add height, so no card becomes unreachable. It fades to `--cds-ui-bg`, so one rule serves both themes. Whether there is more below is **measured, not assumed** (`scrollTop + clientHeight < scrollHeight - 1`, re-measured on scroll and by a feature-detected `ResizeObserver`, re-bound when the confidence step swaps the grid out and back), because how many cards fit depends on the height the station hands down and on the font size. There is no transition, deliberately: this is information, and information must not depend on `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Theme membership is display-only and lives in the picker**, deliberately NOT in @/domain/scene-kinds.ts (that list is generated and never hand-edited). Since ENG-390 the `THEMES` table has exactly one job left: it is the colour lookup behind `tintOf()`, so each card keeps its coloured dot as identity for that kind. It is not dead code — deleting it uncolours all 27 cards — but it no longer renders a heading, a `data-theme` block or an i18n label. Theme tints are the leading entries of `scenePalette` from @/ui/tokens; the fallback for a kind outside every theme is `scenePalette[5]` (a dead path today — every kind is mapped).
- **The coverage drawer is a thin formatter over domain data**: it receives a ready `Coverage` (@/domain/coverage.ts), renders one mono row per kind that has any count (the English contract value + firme/hesitante/alvo counts) plus the absence-candidate chips, whose labels resolve through `sceneKindLabel` (@/ui/i18n). The "1–2" target display for the rare (ALTA) tier is the drawer's presentation decision — domain supplies a single numeric target. Trigger, portal, overlay, focus trap and dismissal (ESC, outside tap, ×) are all Radix Dialog.

### Things to Know

- **Hit-testing survives the scroll because it measures the CONTAINER, never the window**: `beadFromEvent` reads `getBoundingClientRect()` of `.cds-necklace` (the positioned field), whose top slides up as the window scrolls, so `clientY - rect.top` stays a field coordinate. Measuring the window instead would silently offset every click by `scrollTop` — a data-entry bug, not a cosmetic one, which is why `necklace.browser.test.tsx` clicks beads AFTER setting `scrollTop`.
- **Listener-minimalism guards** (PRD v2 §9.2): @/ui/organisms/necklace/minimalism.test.tsx and @/ui/organisms/triage-picker/minimalism.test.tsx assert no digit ever renders as visible text, `aria-label`, `title` or `placeholder` on these listener-facing surfaces — hence copy like "Ver todos os tipos por tema" instead of "os 27 tipos". The coverage drawer is exempt by design: it is a facilitator-density surface where digits are allowed, but it must stay closed by default and fully absent from the document until opened.
- **Test split by file suffix** (@/vitest.config.ts): `*.test.tsx` runs in jsdom (`necklace.test.tsx` asserts the render vocabulary; `geometry.test.ts` covers the pure math), while `*.browser.test.tsx` runs in real Chromium — required here because jsdom has zeroed layout and no real hit-testing. There is **no `vitest-browser-react`** in the repo: browser tests drive the component with raw `createRoot`/`flushSync` and native `dispatchEvent(new PointerEvent(...))` for coordinate hit-testing.
- The 280 ms hover-edge dwell (a single edge preview fires only after the pointer rests near a selection edge) is tested with `vi.useFakeTimers()` + `advanceTimersByTimeAsync`; the single-listener claim is verified by spying on `addEventListener`. Touch pointers skip hover entirely.
- `ResizeObserver` is feature-detected — absent in jsdom, where the one synchronous width measure suffices; in the browser it re-measures on resize.
- Pearl (@/ui/atoms) stays non-clickable: the organism owns all pointer handling, matching the necklace performance pattern described in @/ui/docs.md. The scene-end square, dim margin and selection edge-ring are all Pearl `data-*` states driven from `computeField`.
- **The seam modal's interaction test is mandatory** and lives in the browser suite, not jsdom — its focus trap is only provable in real Chromium (jsdom has no native Tab navigation), the same split the necklace uses for its own coordinate click model.
- **`inert` was considered for the recording lock and rejected** (ENG-393). It typechecks fine on `@types/react` 19.x (`inert?: boolean`) and is the semantically right tool for a transient group freeze, but Chrome suppresses click dispatch inside an inert subtree entirely — no event reaches the element or any ancestor. That would make every refused tap silent, and on an ear-first screen the refusal IS the answer (the page wires `onBlocked` to `UiSound.refuse()`). Native `disabled` has the same defect. Hence `aria-disabled` plus handler refusal, and — for the `header` slot, which arrives as an opaque `ReactNode` with no handler to wrap — an `onClickCapture` guard on the wrapper that stops propagation before the click reaches whatever is inside.
- **The seam modal deliberately does NOT render the domain's `question`/`warning` strings**: those carry digits (the WHY_DELTA copy in `BORDER_COPY`, @/domain/seam.ts), and this is a listener-facing surface (§9.2). It shows the redesign headline, the PRD button copy and the `simple`-only consequence line, all from @/ui/i18n; `delta`/`thr` stay internal. A side effect worth knowing: because it renders no domain copy, the seam modal is one of the few surfaces that is **fully** translated — the domain error/gate copy elsewhere stays PT-BR. Each action carries a stable `key` (`move`/`reanchor`/…) because the translated label can no longer identify the focus-target button. A minimalism test sweeps every offer variant for leaked digits in text, `aria-label` and `title`.
- **Radix Dialog conventions** (set by the seam modal, continued by the coverage drawer): `Dialog.Title` is mandatory (Radix warns/errors without it); with no `Dialog.Description`, pass `aria-describedby={undefined}` on `Dialog.Content` so it doesn't reference a nonexistent id; slide/fade animations live ONLY inside the `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` guard — without them Radix unmounts the panel instantly, which is the correct base behavior, so reduced-motion needs no extra handling. `Dialog.Portal` renders to `document.body`, so tests query via `screen`/`document`, never the render container. The modal Dialog defers pointer-down-outside dismissal to the subsequent `click` event (`deferPointerDownOutside`), so outside-dismiss tests must fire pointerdown AND click — and the outside listener attaches in a `setTimeout(0)`, so give the tick first (same quirk recorded in @/ui/organisms/coverage-drawer/coverage-drawer.test.tsx).
- **Roving tabindex without refs:** `KindCard` (@/ui/molecules) does not forward a ref, so the picker's keyboard handling queries `[role="radio"]` in DOM order via `querySelectorAll` on the group container, finds `document.activeElement` among them and focuses the next one. Exactly one card is `tabbable` at a time (the `tabbable` prop), tracked as a focused index clamped when the radio count changes (filter/disclosure).
- **The picker is one single radiogroup** spanning commons + theme sections + the none-fit card, with theme headings as purely visual labels: nesting `role=group` inside a `radiogroup` is not sanctioned by ARIA (a radiogroup's required owned children are radios). Arrow keys move focus WITHOUT selecting (the APG toolbar variant, not the standard radio move-and-select) — selecting reveals the confidence step and destroys the grid, so select-on-arrow would be destructive.
- **Context override, not a new variant**: inside the olive panel the Button atom's ghost variant (olive-on-cream by default) is recolored to a cream outline by a CSS rule scoped under `.cds-seam-modal` — a look-layer override per prototype 1g, leaving the @/ui/atoms Button untouched.
- **prefers-reduced-motion is honored throughout**: every decorative animation in this folder (seam-slide, drawer slide/fade, picker motion) sits under the `no-preference` guard, asserted in tests via `?raw` CSS imports.
- **Sibling organisms import each other by direct path, never the barrel** — the same rule atoms and molecules follow, to keep each pulled module's internals encapsulated and avoid a cycle through @/ui/organisms/index.ts.
- **Shell-only organisms stay out of the barrel**: the connection gate and the tutorial popup are deliberately NOT exported from @/ui/organisms/index.ts — only @/ui/app consumes them, by direct path. Adding them to the barrel would invite station pages to mount shell chrome.
- **First localStorage use in the repo** is the tutorial popup's permanent dismiss: a namespaced, versioned key (`colar-de-sons:tutorial:dismissed:v1`) with every get/set wrapped in try/catch and silent degradation (blocked storage → the dismissal only lasts the session, the app never breaks). New persistent-preference keys should follow this precedent; rationale in @/ui/organisms/tutorial-popup/README.md.
- **Radix Popover's Popper needs `ResizeObserver`**, which jsdom lacks — the tutorial-popup tests stub a no-op class in `beforeAll`. Unlike the seam modal (portal to body, focus trap only provable in Chromium), the popup is fully testable in jsdom because it renders inline and traps no focus.
- **Each session card exposes exactly one interactive element**: a native button named by composition — the visible verb ("Retomar"/"Abrir") plus a visually-hidden span carrying the story title, so screen readers hear "Retomar/Abrir <título>". The whole card is never clickable and no interactives nest — clicking chooses `onResume` for in-progress sessions and `onOpen` for concluded ones. This is also why the status label is a plain `<span>` with a dot and NOT the `Chip` atom (@/ui/atoms), which renders a `<button>`: a status is not an action. The dashed new-story card is the one card that IS a button, and it only exists when `onNew` is supplied.
- **The card cover is a decorative miniature, not a real necklace**: a fixed 22-bead field (two rows of 11) whose lit count is `round(progress × 22)` — clamped to 0..1 — and whose tints cycle @/ui/tokens `scenePalette` every four beads. That grouping is visual rhythm only; it has nothing to do with the story's actual bead grid or scene spans (@/domain). It renders as ONE `role="img"` named by `progressLabel`, so the glance is announced as a single sentence rather than 22 anonymous beads, and no card ever nests a list.
- **There is no Claude Design prototype for the dashboard** — the session list's visual derives from the prototypes' white-card pattern plus @/ui/tokens, and where the prototype implies data the contract does not have (a full name + e-mail, an "Em revisão" status, a "duplicar" the port never grew), the contract wins and the affordance is simply absent. The rest of the prototype's kebab arrived once the port could back it — rename and delete in ENG-281 — and shrank again in ENG-689 when the download entry (ENG-305) left with the artifacts it downloaded.
- **The connection gate only shows the affordance; the actual freeze lives elsewhere.** Its overlay covers the editing surface but keeps children mounted so state is preserved, and the traveling player (@/ui/app/player-slot.tsx) sits in its own layer outside the overlay so playback survives an outage. The mutation block itself is the `apply` no-op in @/ui/state (see @/ui/state/docs.md), fed by the same offline signal.
